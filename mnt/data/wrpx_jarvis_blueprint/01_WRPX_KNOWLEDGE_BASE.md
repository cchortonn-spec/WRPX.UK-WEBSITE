# What WRPX is

WRPX is a specialist architectural vinyl wrapping and surface refurbishment business.

The business started strongly around domestic kitchen wrapping, kitchen worktops, cupboards, drawers, panels, and interior vinyl finishes.

The future direction is bigger:

- commercial architectural wraps
- retail and brand projects
- offices
- hospitality
- leisure sites
- apartment blocks
- landlords and property managers
- subcontracting
- larger refurbishment projects
- repeat commercial clients

WRPX should be positioned as a specialist surface transformation company, not just a kitchen wrapping company.

# Current lead workflow

Typical domestic lead flow:

1. Lead comes from Facebook, website, WhatsApp, email, or contact form.
2. Connor tries to move conversation to WhatsApp Business where possible.
3. Customer is asked what they want doing:
   - whole kitchen
   - doors/drawers only
   - worktops only
   - both cupboards and worktops
   - other surface
4. Customer is asked what colour or style they have in mind.
5. Customer sends photos.
6. Connor either prices from photos or books a survey/measure.
7. Quote or estimate is sent.
8. Follow-up is needed.
9. If accepted, deposit invoice is created.
10. Materials are ordered.
11. Install is scheduled.
12. Final invoice is sent.
13. Review follow-up is sent.

Jarvis should structure and support this entire flow.

# Pricing philosophy

Connor often calculates material first, then adds his fee/labour/profit.

Pricing should consider:

- material cost

# Materials and calculations

Jarvis will eventually help calculate:

- vinyl metres for cupboard doors
- vinyl metres for drawers
- vinyl metres for panels
- vinyl metres for plinths
- vinyl metres for worktops
- extra allowance
- roll wastage
- MDF sheets
- hinge packs
- cut patterns
- patterned vinyl direction
- plain vinyl optimisation

Important rules:

- Patterned/textured vinyl may need grain direction maintained.
- Plain vinyl can usually rotate more freely.
- Worktops need extra width/length allowance.
- Cupboard doors/panels usually need wrap allowance around edges.
- Connor often adds extra metres as a safety allowance.

Future AI/cutting tools should ask for clarification if measurements or material direction are unclear.

---

# MDF cutting future requirement

Jarvis should eventually calculate new door cutting from 4x8 ft MDF boards.

Common board size:

- 4 ft x 8 ft sheet
- approximately 1220 mm x 2440 mm
- common thickness: 18 mm for doors, 6 mm for some panels/boiler boxes

Future feature should:

- calculate number of MDF sheets required
- generate cut layout
- label each part
- colour-code cuts
- minimise waste
- prioritise easiest/quickest cut pattern where appropriate
- allow blade kerf/cut loss
- allow grain direction if board finish requires it

This is not built yet, but all measurement storage should support it later.
